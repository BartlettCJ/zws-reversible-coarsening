\begin{frontnotice}
This is the computational companion to \emph{Zero Without Stasis}. It reports one finite formal example, a reversible artificial world whose signed total stays exactly zero while it organises, and grades every claim by the class of evidence behind it. A later causal inference from the same programme was withdrawn when its detector failed under intervention; the correction is recorded here rather than hidden. The abstract opens in plain language.
\end{frontnotice}

\section*{Abstract}
We found a simple artificial world in which random material reorganises into large, irregular material-rich and near-empty regions while the net signed charge stays exactly zero and every microscopic step can be undone. The result shows how substantial organisation can develop without breaking the global balance or discarding microscopic information; in this model, its timing depends strongly on initial density and its expression depends on the local reactions. We study a deterministic four-state block cellular automaton, whose cells are empty, positive, negative or bound-neutral, with a frozen 256-entry local permutation, selected in an exploratory search and then held fixed throughout the follow-up experiments. Each update conserves the signed charge $Q=N_{+}-N_{-}$ (positive minus negative cells) but not the occupancy $M=N_{+}+N_{-}+N_{\mathrm b}$ (all non-empty cells); the studied worlds start in the global $Q=0$ sector. A $4096\times4096$ localised-patch trajectory developed irregular domains and, after 65,536 forward phases and the corresponding inverse phases, recovered all 16,777,216 cells exactly. Separate $1024\times1024$ homogeneous-random ensembles, with 12 seeds per density, exhibited density-sensitive domain formation with high local compositional disorder. No run at initial occupancy 0.16 crossed the fixed geometrical endpoint (a specified growth of connected empty regions, defined in Section~4) by 262,144 phases; at 0.18, 8/12 crossed by 524,288 phases. The Kaplan--Meier median first passage decreased from 495,616 phases at 0.18 to 65,536 at 0.26. Event-centred morphology favoured distributed change rather than a localised droplet front. In the pooled control protocol, an active sibling conserving both $Q$ and $M$ and a pure-rotation rule each gave 0/16 end-of-window strong-domain passes, versus 10/16 for the selected rule; several single reaction-pair removals also suppressed them. The contribution is this reproducible, quantitatively controlled finite-time regime, not reversibility or signed balance alone; the data are finite-time and do not establish an equilibrium phase transition.

\textbf{Keywords:} reversible cellular automata; Margolus block automata; signed charge conservation; coarsening; first passage; classifier correction

\begin{sigbox}{Plain-language significance}
Reversible rules do not prevent macroscopic structure, but dramatic pictures alone do not identify a mechanism. This study pairs an exact cell-for-cell reversal test with waiting-time ensembles, density sweeps, arrangement controls, a matched double-conservation sibling, and reaction-pair ablations. The central result is deliberately narrow: one fully specified reversible, signed-charge-conserving automaton produces delayed, large-scale, irregular organisation from random material, and that behaviour is strongly conditioned by density and by the reaction architecture tested. All trajectories remain exactly recoverable in principle and in the large direct audit.
\end{sigbox}

\section{Introduction}
This paper is the computational companion to \emph{Zero Without Stasis} \citep{Bartlett2026ZWS}, which proposes exactly this kind of experiment (its Section 14.1, \emph{Signed Life}) and states in advance what a simulation can and cannot show for that framework. The present study stands on its own as a finite computational result; the parent paper's metaphysical claims are neither assumed nor tested here. The model is a finite, fixed-lattice realisation of that Signed Life programme; it does not implement the distinct splitting-and-merging refinement conjecture of the parent paper's Sections 6.4 and 14.2, which motivated the work.

Reversible cellular automata and lattice gases have long supplied constructive examples of local invertibility, conservation, and emergent statistical behaviour \citep{FredkinToffoli1982,Margolus1984,Vichniac1984,ToffoliMargolus1987,ToffoliMargolus1990}. Conservative lattice gases recover transport and hydrodynamic phenomena \citep{HPP1973,FrischHasslacherPomeau1986,RothmanZaleski1994,BoonEtAl1996}; reversible Ising and Q2R models display relaxation, long cycles, and phase-like macroscopic behaviour \citep{Creutz1986,Takesue1987,UrbinaRica2016,Urbina2020,MontalvaMedelRicaUrbina2020}; and reversible aggregation shows that invertible local dynamics can support cluster growth \citep{DSouzaMargolus1999}. More recent one-dimensional reversible automata with vacancies and two particle species provide close alphabet, reaction, and conserved-charge precedents \citep{MedenjakPopkovProsen2019,KlobasProsen2022}, while reactive lattice-gas automata provide established examples of waves and pattern formation \citep{KapralLawniczakMasiar1991}. We therefore make no claim that reversibility plus organisation, multi-species reversible dynamics, or reaction-driven patterning is new in itself.

The narrower question is what can be established for a four-state signed system in which one additive quantity is exact while total occupancy can change. The distinction matters. A rule may conserve
\begin{equation}
Q=N_{+}-N_{-},
\end{equation}
without conserving
\begin{equation}
M=N_{+}+N_{-}+N_{\mathrm b}.
\end{equation}
Writing ``$Q=0$'' describes the global sector of the runs, not a second local conservation law and not an empty world. This paper asks whether a frozen rule (one selected in the exploratory search and then held fixed, never re-tuned, through all follow-up experiments) in that architecture reproducibly forms large irregular domains, how density and waiting time condition the outcome, and which nearby rule controls retain or suppress it.

Our terminology is descriptive. ``Coarsening-like'' refers to growth of long-wavelength structure and connected regions over the observed finite lattice and time window. ``Strong-domain'' refers to a fixed operational endpoint. Neither term asserts equilibrium phase separation, a critical point, a free-energy description, or a universality class \citep{HohenbergHalperin1977,Bray1994}.

\section{Model and exact audits}
\subsection{Four-state partitioned dynamics}
Each square-lattice cell takes a value in $\{0,+,-,\mathrm b\}$: empty, positive excitation, negative excitation, or bound-neutral excitation. Alternating $2\times2$ Margolus partitions tile the periodic lattice. The four input symbols of each block are packed into one of 256 block states; a 256-entry permutation maps that block state to an output state. Applying the inverse permutation in reverse phase order recovers the predecessor exactly. The frozen winner's raw unsigned-byte rule table has SHA-256
\begin{center}
\code{d1fe99b5735d8e9be032347de2727dc5c52a7848df200ca26f67f28c8c1bc41c}.
\end{center}
All 256 local cases were exhaustively audited. For every block input, the permutation is one-to-one and preserves the block contribution to $Q$. The winner does not preserve the block contribution to $M$; bound/unbound and occupied/empty reaction channels allow occupancy to change while signed balance remains exact. The construction starts from clockwise block rotation and exchanges 12 reversible pairs of outputs, so 24 of the 256 input blocks differ from pure rotation. Figure~\ref{fig:rule-worked} shows the alternating partition and two entries from the archived table, including an actual reaction in which a balanced positive--negative pair is added while $Q$ is unchanged.

\subsection{Large exact reversal}
The audited $4096\times4096$ trajectory began with a random central square spanning 45\% of each lattice dimension, with the rest empty. Within the patch the target state fractions were 12\% positive, 12\% negative and 8\% bound-neutral, with exact positive--negative balance. It was advanced for 65,536 partition phases and then evolved backwards with the exact inverse schedule. The recovered lattice matched its initial state at all 16,777,216 cells. Global $Q$ remained zero throughout; $M$ changed substantially. This is an implementation and coexistence audit, not a claim that reversibility itself is novel.

\section{Discovery, selection, and confirmation}
\subsection{A small, explicitly selective search}
The winner was search-selected, not sampled blindly. The original discovery run, a small-scale exploratory search (a ``smoke'' run in the software's terms), evaluated 14 unique rules (16 replicate evaluations) in a reactive-rotation construction family. Its declared heuristic rewarded intermediate bands of activity, reaction density, state and block entropy, spatial and temporal mutual information, recurrence, perturbation distance, and patch repetition, with a dynamics gate and a hard charge-preservation gate. Thus subsequent behaviour cannot be described as wholly unselected.

The discovery score did \emph{not} use the later $4096^2$ reversal result, density first-passage curves, event morphology, arrangement controls, double-conservation sibling, reaction-pair ablations, prevalence screen, or the later classifier diagnostics. Those were frozen and run as follow-up measurements. The exact search provenance and metric weights are retained with the release.

\subsection{Separation of evidence classes}
We use four labels. \emph{Discovery} denotes the tiny search and exploratory smoke run. \emph{Confirmation} denotes frozen winner repeats and the large exact reversal. \emph{Mechanism constraints} denote preregistered density, geometry, sibling, and ablation comparisons. \emph{Diagnostics} denote analyses that reveal an assay limitation and cannot support their original causal question. Results below state their class rather than pooling them as if they were exchangeable.

The later prevalence screen found 19 strong-domain rules among 256 independently generated charge-preserving candidates (7.4219\%; Wilson 95\% interval 4.8026--11.3001\%). This shows that the operational regime is not unique to one table within the sampled generator, but it neither erases the original selection nor estimates prevalence in all $256!$ block permutations.

\section{Measurements}
The v0.3/v0.3.1 strong-domain endpoint is a geometrical, trajectory-prefix classifier; it does \emph{not} include a spectral-growth threshold. In words, the flag fires once the finite (non-wrapping) empty regions have grown, at some past sample, to at least four times their initial characteristic scale, still stand at least twice that scale at the current sample, the largest such region at that peak sample covers at least 0.1\% of the lattice, and the global charge has never changed. Formally, let $L_j$ be the measured characteristic scale of finite empty components at sampled phase $t_j$, let $r_j=L_j/\max(L_0,1)$, and let $j^*$ be the first index attaining the largest $r_j$ in the prefix through $t_k$. Writing $f_j$ for the lattice fraction in the largest finite empty component, the implemented flag is
\begin{equation}
 C_k=\mathbf{1}\!\left[
 r_{j^*}\geq4,\quad r_k\geq2,\quad f_{j^*}\geq0.001,
 \quad Q_j=Q_0\ \hbox{for all }j\leq k\right].
 \label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ equals 1 when every listed condition holds and 0 otherwise. A prefix requires at least two snapshots. Spectral power, interface density, anisotropy and spatial mutual information are \emph{separate measurements}, not additional gates in Eq.~\ref{eq:endpoint}. This distinction is essential to the later shuffled-state false positives.

For the v0.3.1 first-passage study, $T_{\rm hit}$ is the first sampled phase with $C_k=1$. The separate persistent endpoint, $T_{\rm persistent}$, is the first sample starting three consecutive true flags. Samples are 2,048 phases apart. A transient first hit need not be the persistent hit. Runs that had not crossed by the end of their follow-up (right-censored runs) are retained as such rather than assigned artificial event times, and Kaplan--Meier medians are reported only when reached \citep{KaplanMeier1958,RoystonParmar2013}. Earlier campaigns used related but distinct frozen endpoints; the Supplement states their differences rather than treating all reported pass counts as interchangeable.

All quantitative claims refer to the stored sizes, seeds, phase horizons, and frozen thresholds. The endpoint is useful for comparing these runs but is not a natural law. Exact local-state images are rendered with nearest-neighbour sampling. Reported ``reaction density'' is the fraction of updated blocks that change reaction channel; it is not a chemical rate constant.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Large-scale order with local compositional disorder}
The localised-patch studies and the later whole-torus homogeneous-random studies both showed broad excitation-rich regions separated by near-vacuum regions, under their respective preparation and density conditions. The numerical composition result here comes from the four $4096\times4096$ localised-patch confirmation runs: the median occupancy of the dense interior (cells in the high-local-density band, excluding a margin around the dense/sparse interface) at the final stored snapshot of each run was 0.572989, with positive, negative and bound-neutral fractions 0.192542, 0.192535 and 0.187912. Normalised four-state compositional entropy was 0.946343. High single-site diversity is not itself proof of spatial randomness; the images and separately recorded spectral diagnostics support the description of irregular, noncrystalline morphology.

Across those four localised-patch confirmatory winner runs, the transient domain-scale peak ranged from 72.66 to 136.11 times its initial value (median 98.4459), while the final recorded scale remained 15.77--16.33 times initial (median 16.1118). The effect is large but nonmonotone: the preregistered sustained late-time coarsening criterion (a separate, stricter test that also required continued growth of the empty-domain scale through the late half of the run; Supplement, Section~5.3) was not met at this scale. The large transient morphology is not a success of that stronger hypothesis.

\subsection{Density controls waiting time}
The first-passage cohort comprised homogeneous-random $1024\times1024$ worlds. At an initial occupancy of 0.16, none of 12 runs reached the endpoint within 262,144 phases. At 0.18, 8/12 reached it in the extended 524,288-phase campaign, with median first passage 495,616 phases. All 12 replicates reached it at each tested density from 0.20 through 0.26; median first passage fell from 321,536 phases at 0.20 to 65,536 at 0.26 (Table~\ref{tab:density}). Within the v0.3/v0.3.1 endpoint, longer observation admitted events at densities that rarely crossed at shorter times. The earlier approximately 27.5\% onset came from a related but different frozen classifier and is not a numerically interchangeable threshold estimate. The defensible result is strong finite-time density dependence, not an equilibrium critical density.

\subsection{Distributed events and the prepared-geometry comparison}
Each endpoint crossing was examined in a frozen window of the samples leading up to it and labelled by where the change happened: \emph{localised} if at least half of the sites whose occupancy changed lay inside one fixed crop covering a sixteenth of the lattice (a single growing nucleus), \emph{distributed} if that crop held no more than an eighth of the changed sites while the finite-empty scale grew in at least half of the pre-hit intervals, and \emph{unresolved} otherwise (two further preregistered labels, merger and reconnection, matched no event). The pool is the 91 first hits observed in the v0.3.1 study, 68 from the primary $1024^2$ cohort and 23 from the $512^2$ area-scaling cohort. The labels classified 67 transitions as distributed, 24 as unresolved, and none as localised droplet-front events. Under a simple local Poisson picture, in which independent nucleation events occur at a fixed rate per cell, the first-event rate per cell would be the same on the matched $512^2$ and $1024^2$ lattices; instead the smaller lattice showed roughly four times the per-cell rate at every matched density, so that model was not supported. These finite-window observations constrain a localised-front interpretation without excluding every nucleation description or establishing an asymptotic rate law.

Prepared starts that place the same occupied inventory in a single compact disc (a droplet) or a single full-height band (a slab), at a set internal density with the rest of the torus empty, produced 0/48 endpoint crossings by 262,144 phases, versus 19/24 for matched homogeneous starts, while their imposed occupied structures persisted or grew. Crucially, Eq.~\ref{eq:endpoint} measures finite empty-component growth relative to each trajectory's own initial finite-empty-domain scale; it is not an occupied-seed survival test. Preparing a large occupied shape changes the starting geometry and its normalization baseline relative to random soup. The pass-count contrast is not a common absolute test of whether a large structure forms or survives, and it cannot by itself show that seeded organisation fails or that compact seeds inhibit the phenomenon. The direct persistence measurements and the relative-growth endpoint must be read separately.

\subsection{Conservation and reaction controls constrain the mechanism}
The closest reactive sibling is a variant of the winner altered as little as possible so that every block update conserves $M$ as well as $Q$ while keeping nonzero reactions. It remained dynamically active (median across runs of the mean reaction density 0.01160, versus 0.01234 for the winner) but produced 0/8 strong-domain outcomes in the focused $1024^2$ screen (8 replicates) and 0/16 end-of-window strong-domain passes in the pooled $512^2$ balanced protocol (16 replicates); the winner produced 8/8 and 10/16 respectively. A pure-rotation control also produced 0/16. Thus inactivity alone does not explain the sibling result, and transport alone is insufficient under this protocol.

Single reaction-pair removals were heterogeneous. Six of twelve tested pair removals produced no end-of-window pass in any of their 16 replicates; the other removals retained 2--7 end-of-window passes. These pooled counts are taken at the end of the observation window and are not first-passage counts: a run can cross the endpoint and later fall back below its final scale-ratio condition, which happened to one run each in two of the ablations. The architecture is therefore distributed across several channels rather than reducible, from these data, to one uniquely necessary pair. \section{Discussion}
The experiments support a compact statement: a fully specified invertible block rule, restricted to the globally charge-balanced sector, can reorganise random material into enormous irregular structures after broad, density-dependent waiting times, while remaining exactly reversible cell for cell. The endpoint is accompanied by substantial long-wave growth but high local compositional entropy. Distributed event morphology, an active exact-$Q{+}M$ sibling, transport-only controls, and reaction-pair ablations narrow the plausible mechanism without reducing it to a single reaction or established thermodynamic theory.

The closest precedents are substantive. Partitioned reversible automata supply the architecture \citep{Margolus1984,MoritaImai2001}; Q2R work supplies conservative reversible dynamics with macro-level relaxation \citep{UrbinaRica2016,Urbina2020}; reversible aggregation and reversible lattice gases demonstrate aggregation and apparent macroscopic irreversibility under invertible microscopic rules \citep{DSouzaMargolus1999,Broadwell2001}; one-dimensional two-species reversible automata supply especially close vacancy, species, reaction, and conserved-charge precedents \citep{MedenjakPopkovProsen2019,KlobasProsen2022}; and reactive lattice gases and reaction automata supply established pattern-forming contexts \citep{KapralLawniczakMasiar1991,BoonEtAl1996,WeimarBoon1994}. The residual contribution is the audited combination of a two-dimensional four-state signed invariant with variable occupancy, delayed distributed coarsening-like organisation, exact large-lattice reversal, and a frozen hierarchy of density, geometry, sibling, and reaction controls. The author-retained search log records query families, databases and publisher checks, and a stopping rule; it is a focused, bounded novelty review rather than proof of exhaustive priority.

The broad waiting-time distribution invites comparison with rare-event and first-passage analysis \citep{BolhuisDellagoChandler1998,KaplanMeier1958}. Yet the data do not identify a free-energy barrier or stationary rate. Likewise, the separation between exact $Q$ and variable $M$ resembles, only at a conceptual level, theories coupling conserved and nonconserved fields \citep{HohenbergHalperin1977,Bray1994}. No equilibrium measure, temperature, or dynamic universality class has been demonstrated.

\section{Limitations and withdrawn inference}
\begin{corrbox}{The v0.3.2 history-erasure headline}
The later v0.3.2 history-erasure campaign cannot support its original causal headline. Global shuffling removed more than 99\% of the source's long-wave power. Yet the fixed endpoint classified many of the shuffled states as ``strong domain'' immediately or within a few thousand phases, because its connected-empty-cluster terms were confounded by the random high-occupancy geometry: in the primary cohort shuffled 32,768 phases before each source's own first hit (Branch B, 16 replicates), 9 hit at elapsed zero and 14 by 4,096 phases. The intervention audits and raw trajectories remain valid records, but the first-passage contrast is not a valid assay of preserved causal spatial memory. We therefore withdraw claims that the experiment showed a necessary hidden multiscale precursor or history-inhibition effect. Full diagnostics and the immutable historical report are separated in the Supplement and erratum.
\end{corrbox}

More generally, the study uses one selected winner, finite two-dimensional tori, finite horizons, operational morphology thresholds, and a bounded generator prevalence sample. Three readings that the vocabulary might invite are not supported by these finite-time data: that the density dependence is an equilibrium phase transition with a critical density; that the reversible yet directional-looking histories demonstrate a thermodynamic arrow; and that conserving $Q$ is what causes the organisation, rather than a constraint under which it happens to occur.

For each fixed torus, the configuration together with the partition phase evolves by a permutation of a finite state space. Microscopic trajectories are therefore periodic, although their periods were not measured here; the reported coarsening-like behaviour is a finite-time macroscopic episode, not an asymptotic attracting phase.

\section{Reproducibility and evidence integrity}
The release records the raw-uint8 winner hash, exact configurations, seeds, horizons, endpoint definitions, compact CSV summaries, state hashes, figure-source hashes, code version, Windows/CUDA/PyTorch environment, and an independent parity audit. \section{Conclusion}
In plain terms, this artificial world builds large, irregular structures out of random material without ever upsetting its overall plus--minus balance, and its microscopic evolution can be reversed cell for cell. The structures need not be crystals: the dense regions retain high local compositional disorder.

What matters is not the arithmetic of cancellation. It is that a simple, fully specified rule produces a reproducible pattern-forming regime, with long delays that depend strongly on starting density and outcomes that change under targeted reaction controls. Fixing the net balance does not fix the amount of occupied material, its arrangement, or how those arrangements change. The active double-conservation sibling and the reaction-pair removals constrain this particular mechanism; they do not show that signed balance causes organisation or that changing occupancy is necessary in every reversible system.

The study therefore moves beyond a static example of quantities summing to zero to an experimentally characterised example of organised dynamics under that constraint.
